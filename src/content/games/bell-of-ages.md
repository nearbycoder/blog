Alder is eleven. His father Tomas, keeper of the great bell, went to mend it last autumn and never came home. On the morning of the lantern festival, the bell sounds its first warning, and the village falls quiet.

Take up a practice sword and your father's reed flute. Follow the pale paths out of Alder Village into the Whisperwood, up Cinderpeak, and down to the Larkwater Coast. Solve each sanctuary's trial, break its guardian seal, and face the warden at its heart. When three relics rest in the altar, the Bell of Ages offers a crossing that costs seven years of your life.

The Bell of Ages is an original adventure inspired by classic 3D action-adventure games. Its world, characters, story, models, and music are original to the project, and it runs in a browser.

## Sword, shield, and the golden ring

Chain a diagonal cut, a return cut, and a heavier thrust. Damage comes from the blade itself: each swing traces the sword's real path against enemy hit volumes, so a miss is really a miss, and a blade that hits a wall glances off in sparks. The sword leaves a warm arc that fades toward the hilt, and hits land with amber sparks and a brief hit-stop. Guardians telegraph every attack with a pulsing golden ring, outlined in dark so it reads on sand and snow, and an arrow on the screen's edge points to one winding up out of view. Guard a blow and the attacker staggers. Lock-on keeps a single foe in focus.

Guardians come in three kinds: the stone **guardian**, the horned **skirmisher** that lunges down a short marked lane, and the tall **warder** that lobs a stone at a circle marked where you stand. Each sanctuary ends with a warden, and every warden has signature attacks with their own warnings on the ground: a **charge** down a marked lane, a **shockwave** that rolls outward from a ring, and a **volley** of three circles that erupt around you.

Seven sanctuaries each run from a puzzle chamber to a sealed guardian hall to a warden's arena, and each asks something different: touch memory stones in the order the roots remember, push a stone onto a seal, echo a melody on the three-note reed flute, turn star mirrors toward the north, balance light and shadow across three flames, or ring bells in the order an inscription names. Each hall and arena has its own layout of cover, and every hall hides a cracked wall that breaks open onto an alcove and a carving for your journal.

The kingdom map remembers the chests and wandering lights you've passed, lets you set your own marker, and the compass always names where the journey goes next. Treasure chests, Mira's three wandering lights, and a star-forged blade wait off the beaten path.

## A kingdom in two ages

Before the crossing, you make your oldest friend Mira a promise: "I'll find my way home" or "I'll remember us as we are." The choice changes your reunion, the memories in your journal, and the ending. As an adult you're taller and stronger and carry the keeper's longsword. The kingdom's light has changed, and new sanctuaries wake in its far corners.

## Graphics fidelity, input and comfort

One **Graphics fidelity** slider has four steps and applies at once. **Low** is for weak graphics, with no post-processing. **Medium**, the default, adds contact shadows and FXAA and lowers its 3D resolution under load. **High** adds a soft bloom, a gentle colour grade and fuller grass. **Ultra** adds supersampling, SMAA, 4096 shadows redrawn every frame, stronger bloom, denser sparks, and depth of field behind the title and story scenes; its extra effects download only when it's first chosen. On the integrated Radeon it was measured on, Low to High took about 2 to 7 ms a frame and Ultra about 15 ms at 1280×800.

The whole campaign plays with keyboard and mouse, a keyboard alone, a standard gamepad, or touch, and prompts follow whichever you used last, with PlayStation, Nintendo or Xbox button names. Keys and buttons can be remapped. Settings include reduced motion, larger interface text, off-screen attack warnings, a toggle shield, captured mouse look, a camera that trails behind you on a gamepad or touch, a left-handed touch layout with larger buttons, and full screen. Every menu works with the keyboard alone, and buttons light on hover, dip when pressed and share one gold focus ring. Scene changes fade through a dark veil instead of cutting.

## What it's built with

The game is TypeScript on **Three.js** r186, built with Vite into plain static files. A few engineering choices do most of the work:

- **One height function.** Terrain rendering, character grounding, and camera clearance all sample the same deterministic function, so nothing floats or sinks.
- **Swept collision.** Movement and dodge rolls are swept circles against rotated boxes and tree trunks in a spatial hash, so fast rolls can't tunnel through fences, and you slide along walls instead of sticking.
- **Blade-accurate combat.** The sword's base-to-tip segment is sampled at 120 Hz across each swing and tested against enemy capsules, so hits that fall between frames still count.
- **A camera that respects walls.** The follow camera casts against obstacle heights, retracts immediately, eases back out, and fades Alder when a wall pushes it close.
- **Rendering on a budget.** Static scenery is batched by material and spatial cell, vegetation is instanced, wind-animated in the shader, and swapped to lighter versions at a distance.

There are no audio files. Every sound, from flute notes to sword swings, is synthesized with the Web Audio API.

## How it was made

All the models, including cottages, the sanctuary, a dungeon kit, props, trees, characters, and the wardens, come from one deterministic Blender Python script, along with their procedural texture atlas. glTF Transform then compresses the export with Meshopt and WebP into a small runtime pack. Two painted textures were made with an AI image-generation tool, with prompts recorded in the repository.

The game was developed with AI coding assistants following OpenAI's guide to [building games with Astra](https://developers.openai.com/blog/how-to-build-games-with-astra). After launch it went through [twelve rounds of improvements](/articles/orchestrating-15-games-with-t3-code/), each run by a fresh AI session and pushed only after its logs were checked. Those rounds found that two of the six treasure chests could never be opened, because a rock and a cliff blocked them, and that the compass led to the back of three sanctuary doors. They added gamepad and touch play, remapping, three journeys, the hearts' low-health warning, the guardian kinds, the wardens' signature attacks, hidden alcoves and the fidelity slider. The tests now include 155 Vitest unit tests and scripted browser checks, including the opening played from the title with real input, and a scripted fighter that measures every hall and arena.

The trailer and screenshots are reproducible. A Playwright harness runs the game under a fake clock, so every frame advances the simulation by exactly 1/30 second, captures each frame in full at Ultra, and records the game's own Web Audio output through an offline audio context.

## Play it

The Bell of Ages is a playable prototype with a complete story from opening to epilogue. [The v0.1.0 web build](https://github.com/nearbycoder/Ocarina/releases/latest) is the October 4 launch build, without any of the improvements above; to play the current game, clone the repository and run `npm install`, then `npm run build && npm run preview` (Node 22.12 or newer). There's no hosted build yet. The README is upfront about its limits: all seven sanctuaries share one three-chamber spine, every enemy uses one reshaped model, combat is forgiving, and gamepad and touch have only been tested with synthetic input.
